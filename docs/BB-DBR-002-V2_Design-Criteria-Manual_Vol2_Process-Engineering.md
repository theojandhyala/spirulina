## 1. Purpose and Scope

This volume establishes the process engineering design criteria for the
BlueBloom Spirulina Production Facility. It defines the quantitative basis on
which process equipment shall be sized, selected, procured and operated.

This volume covers:

- production capacity basis;
- cultivation design criteria;
- culture medium and nutrient design;
- carbon supply;
- inoculum production and culture scale-up;
- water balance;
- harvesting;
- dewatering and washing;
- drying;
- milling, sieving and blending;
- tablet production;
- packaging;
- product specification;
- contamination control;
- process monitoring and control;
- process utilities and energy demand;
- effluent and waste streams;
- process safety;
- overall mass balance.

This volume does not cover civil, structural, architectural, electrical or
instrumentation detailed design, which are addressed in their own volumes and
specifications.

## 3. Basis of Process Design

### 3.1 Status of the Figures in this Volume

The quantities in this volume are a **preliminary design basis**. They are
internally consistent and are suitable for equipment sizing, budgeting and
procurement enquiry, but they are derived from assumed values for pond area,
areal productivity and operating days.

Every assumed value is listed in the Assumptions Register at Section 24, together
with the study, trial or survey that confirms it. No assumed value shall be
carried into a purchase order for major equipment without either confirmation
per Section 24 or an accepted engineering margin covering the uncertainty.

### 3.2 Design Philosophy for Process Capacity

Two distinct capacity figures are used throughout, and they shall not be
interchanged:

| Figure | Purpose | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Peak design capacity** | Sizing of all process equipment, piping, utilities and storage | Best-day areal productivity |
| **Annual mean capacity** | Production planning, revenue forecasting, raw-material budgeting, warehouse sizing | Annual mean areal productivity across operating days |

Equipment sized on annual mean capacity will be undersized on the best days of
the year and will constrain output at exactly the point where the culture is
most productive. Production forecasts based on peak capacity will overstate
annual output by a factor of approximately 1.7. Both errors shall be avoided.

The derived figures shall remain mutually consistent. At Stage 1 peak:

| Check | Value | Comment |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Daily production as % of standing stock | 9.2 %/day | Within the 5–15%/day range typical of outdoor raceway culture |
| Daily harvest volume as % of pond volume | 10.0 %/day | Within the 8–20%/day range typical of commercial practice |

A revision to pond area, depth, productivity or harvest concentration shall be
checked against both ratios above. A design falling outside these ranges
indicates an inconsistent basis, not an improved one.

| Stage | Site area | Net pond area | Site coverage | Annual dry biomass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

### 4.3 Seasonality and Turndown

Areal productivity in an open raceway is not constant through the year. The
design shall accommodate:

| Condition | Design requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Peak production, clear-sky months | Equipment sized to peak per Section 3.2 |
| Monsoon months | Reduced productivity, dilution risk per Section 9.6, harvest may be suspended |
| Winter minimum temperature | Reduced productivity, harvest frequency reduced |
| Culture loss and pond restart | One pond out of service without loss of the others |
| Cleaning and maintenance | Ponds taken out of service in rotation |

Harvesting, dewatering and drying equipment shall be capable of stable operation
at 30% of peak throughput. Equipment that cannot be turned down to 30% shall be
operated in batch mode rather than continuously derated.

### 4.4 Pond Configuration

| Parameter | Stage 1 basis |
| --- | --- |
| Number of production ponds | 4 |
| Nominal area per production pond | 500 m² |
| Indicative pond dimensions | ≈ 62 m × 8 m overall |
| Channel width | ≈ 3.8 m per channel, two channels per pond |
| Culture volume per pond | 100 m³ |
| Nursery ponds | 2 × 50 m² |
| Freeboard above operating depth | 150 mm minimum |

Production ponds shall be independently isolatable, independently drainable and
independently harvestable. A contamination event or culture crash in one pond
shall not require any other pond to be taken out of service.

Four ponds rather than one or two is a deliberate risk decision: it caps the
loss from any single culture failure at 25% of standing stock and permits
staggered harvesting, at the cost of additional civil works and paddle-wheel
drives.

## 5. Cultivation Design Criteria

### 5.1 Organism

| Item | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| Species | *Arthrospira platensis* |
| Culture type | Open raceway, continuous paddle-wheel circulation, semi-continuous harvest |
| Strain source | To be established from a documented, traceable culture collection |
| Strain records | Strain identity, origin and passage history shall be recorded and retained |

Strain selection shall favour demonstrated performance under local temperature
and light conditions over published laboratory productivity.

### 5.2 Culture Chemistry

| Parameter | Operating target | Operating range | Low alarm | High alarm |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| pH | 9.8 | 9.5 – 10.5 | 8.8 | 11.0 |
| Alkalinity as NaHCO₃ equivalent | 12 g/L | 8 – 16 g/L | 6 g/L | 20 g/L |
| Salinity as TDS | 18 g/L | 12 – 30 g/L | 10 g/L | 35 g/L |
| Dissolved oxygen | — | — | — | Supersaturation to be relieved by circulation |
| Nitrate as N | 80 mg/L | 40 – 200 mg/L | 20 mg/L | — |
| Phosphate as P | 15 mg/L | 8 – 40 mg/L | 4 mg/L | — |

The high pH and high alkalinity of the medium are a **process control measure,
not merely a growth condition**. They are the principal barrier to competing
organisms. Any proposal to operate at reduced pH or reduced alkalinity for
reagent cost reasons shall be treated as a contamination-control deviation and
processed under Volume 1 § 16.

### 5.3 Temperature

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Optimum culture temperature | 35 – 37 °C |
| Satisfactory growth range | 25 – 38 °C |
| Reduced growth, culture maintained | 20 – 25 °C |
| Growth substantially arrested | Below 18 °C |
| Damage threshold | Above 42 °C |
| Culture high-temperature alarm | 40 °C |

Culture temperature shall be monitored in each production pond. Where sustained
culture temperatures above 40 °C are recorded, mitigation shall be assessed,
including increased operating depth, shading, harvest timing and increased
circulation.

Volume 1 § 9 gives a maximum ambient dry-bulb design temperature of 45 °C. The
gap between that ambient figure and the 42 °C culture damage threshold is the
single largest climatic risk to the culture at this site and shall be addressed
explicitly in detailed design, not left to operational discretion.

### 5.4 Light

| Parameter | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| Light saturation | ≈ 150 – 200 µmol/m²/s PAR |
| Behaviour above saturation | Photoinhibition; mitigated by culture density and mixing |
| Design response to high irradiance | Maintain biomass density and circulation rather than reduce exposure |
| Culture depth relationship | Deeper culture increases self-shading and thermal inertia, reduces areal productivity |

Operating depth is a control variable, not a fixed constant. Depth may be
increased in peak summer to buffer temperature and reduce photoinhibition, and
reduced in cooler months to increase light penetration.

### 5.5 Mixing and Circulation

| Parameter | Design basis |
| --- | --- |
| Design channel velocity | 0.25 m/s |
| Acceptable range | 0.20 – 0.30 m/s |
| Minimum velocity to prevent settlement | 0.15 m/s |
| Circulation | Continuous, 24 hours per day |
| Paddle wheel | One per production pond |
| Paddle wheel speed | Selected to achieve design velocity, typically 8 – 15 rpm |
| Specific mixing power | 3 W/m² of pond area (preliminary) |

Circulation shall not be interrupted for cost saving at night. Settlement of
biomass, thermal stratification and localised oxygen accumulation follow within
hours of circulation loss.

Per Volume 1 § 11, paddle-wheel drives shall be connected to backup power.
Paddle-wheel reliability is identified in Volume 1 § 5.3 as a critical
cultivation risk and shall be treated as such in equipment selection: bearing
arrangement, gearbox rating and shaft-seal design shall be specified for
continuous duty in an alkaline, abrasive, outdoor environment.

### 5.6 Biomass Density and Harvest Trigger

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Harvest trigger concentration | 0.65 g/L dry weight |
| Acceptable harvest range | 0.50 – 0.90 g/L |
| Concentration after harvest | ≈ 0.55 g/L |
| Minimum working concentration | 0.30 g/L |
| Routine field measurement | Secchi depth, correlated to dry weight |
| Reference measurement | Gravimetric dry weight, laboratory |

Secchi depth shall be correlated against gravimetric dry weight for the specific
culture at commissioning, and the correlation shall be re-established after any
change of strain, medium or operating depth. A correlation established for one
culture shall not be assumed valid for another.

### 5.7 Control and Alarm Summary

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## 6. Culture Medium and Nutrient Design

### 6.1 Medium Basis

The reference medium is Zarrouk's medium. Full-strength Zarrouk is a laboratory
formulation and is not economic at commercial scale, principally because of its
bicarbonate loading.

The design basis is a **modified Zarrouk medium** in which:

- bicarbonate is reduced and maintained by CO₂ injection per Section 7;
- nitrogen is supplied at a rate matched to uptake rather than held at
  laboratory concentration;
- micronutrients are dosed to a defined schedule.

| Component | Full Zarrouk | Design basis for initial medium make-up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NaHCO₃ | 16.8 g/L | 10 – 12 g/L, maintained by CO₂ |
| NaNO₃ | 2.5 g/L | Dosed to maintain 40 – 200 mg/L as N |
| K₂HPO₄ | 0.5 g/L | 0.25 g/L, dosed to maintain 8 – 40 mg/L as P |
| K₂SO₄ | 1.0 g/L | 0.5 g/L |
| NaCl | 1.0 g/L | 0.5 g/L |
| MgSO₄·7H₂O | 0.2 g/L | 0.2 g/L |
| CaCl₂·2H₂O | 0.04 g/L | 0.04 g/L |
| FeSO₄·7H₂O | 0.01 g/L | 0.01 g/L |
| EDTA | 0.08 g/L | 0.08 g/L |
| Micronutrient solution | Per A5 formulation | Per A5 formulation |

The final medium formulation shall be confirmed by cultivation trial (A-08)
before bulk nutrient procurement. Medium composition shall be adjusted for the
mineral content of the actual source water per Section 9.2 — dosing a standard
formulation into water of unknown hardness, alkalinity or sulphate content will
give an unpredictable medium.

### 6.2 Nutrient Uptake Factors

The following elemental composition of dry *A. platensis* biomass is the design
basis for nutrient demand.

| Element | % of dry biomass | Peak demand at 24 kg/day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Carbon | 46 % | 11.0 kg/day |
| Nitrogen | 10.5 % | 2.52 kg/day |
| Phosphorus | 1.1 % | 0.26 kg/day |
| Potassium | 1.5 % | 0.36 kg/day |
| Sulphur | 0.8 % | 0.19 kg/day |
| Magnesium | 0.3 % | 0.07 kg/day |
| Iron | 0.1 % | 0.024 kg/day |

The nitrogen figure is consistent with a protein content of approximately 65% on
a nitrogen-to-protein factor of 6.25. If a higher protein specification is
adopted per Section 15, the nitrogen demand rises proportionally and the
nutrient balance shall be recalculated.

### 6.3 Nutrient Balance — Peak Day

| Reagent | Element supplied | Peak consumption | 7-day storage (Vol 1 § 15) | Annual at mean output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sodium nitrate (16.5% N) | N | 15.3 kg/day | 107 kg | 2,850 kg |
| *or* Urea (46.6% N) | N | 5.4 kg/day | 38 kg | 1,010 kg |
| Di-potassium phosphate (17.8% P) | P, K | 1.48 kg/day | 10.4 kg | 276 kg |
| Potassium sulphate | K, S | As required for balance | — | — |
| Magnesium sulphate heptahydrate (9.9% Mg) | Mg, S | 0.73 kg/day | 5.1 kg | 136 kg |
| Ferrous sulphate heptahydrate (20.1% Fe) | Fe | 0.12 kg/day | 0.85 kg | 22 kg |
| Sodium bicarbonate | C, alkalinity | Make-up only, per Section 7 | — | — |

Di-potassium phosphate dosed to meet phosphorus demand supplies approximately
0.66 kg/day of potassium against a demand of 0.36 kg/day. Potassium sulphate
addition shall therefore be reduced accordingly and shall not be dosed to the
full Zarrouk figure, or potassium will accumulate.

**Nitrogen source selection.** Urea is markedly cheaper per kilogram of nitrogen
and reduces sodium loading, but it hydrolyses to ammonia, which is toxic to the
culture at elevated pH and which is lost to atmosphere. Sodium nitrate is stable,
predictable and directly available to the culture, at higher cost and with
higher sodium input to the medium. The selection shall be made on trial results
(A-08), not on reagent price alone. Where urea is adopted, dosing shall be
frequent and dilute, and free ammonia shall be added to the monitoring schedule
of Section 5.7.

### 6.4 Dosing Philosophy

| Requirement | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| Dosing mode | Little and often, in solution, into a circulating pond |
| Prohibited | Addition of undissolved solids directly to a production pond |
| Preparation | Dedicated nutrient preparation tank with agitation |
| Dosing point | Downstream of the paddle wheel, into the moving stream |
| Verification | Nutrient concentration confirmed by laboratory analysis, not by dose record alone |
| Interlock | Dosing inhibited when circulation is not running |

The dosing interlock is a safety requirement as well as a process one:
concentrated nutrient dosed into a static pond creates a local zone of high
concentration and extreme pH.

### 6.5 Chemical Storage

Per Volume 1 § 15, critical chemical storage shall be a minimum of seven days,
subject to safety limits. Storage shall additionally account for:

- monsoon-season delivery disruption;
- minimum economic order quantity from the supplier;
- shelf life and caking behaviour of hygroscopic reagents;
- segregation of incompatible materials;
- separation of food-grade nutrient reagents from cleaning chemicals.

Nutrient reagents in contact with the culture shall be of a grade suitable for
food production. Technical or fertiliser-grade reagents shall not be substituted
without documented review of heavy-metal content, since heavy metals concentrate
in the biomass and are subject to the product limits of Section 15.

## 7. Carbon Supply and CO₂ System

### 7.1 Carbon Demand

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Carbon in dry biomass | 46 % |
| Peak carbon demand | 11.0 kg C/day |
| Stoichiometric CO₂ demand | 40.5 kg CO₂/day |
| Design CO₂ utilisation efficiency | 65 % |
| Delivered CO₂ requirement, peak | 62 kg/day |
| Design basis for supply and storage | 70 kg/day |

### 7.2 Supply Philosophy

Carbon shall be supplied as CO₂ injected into a bicarbonate-buffered medium. The
bicarbonate reservoir buffers the culture against short interruptions in CO₂
supply and against pH excursions; the CO₂ regenerates bicarbonate consumed by
biomass growth.

Sodium bicarbonate shall be dosed only as make-up for carbon lost with harvested
biomass and with blowdown, not as the primary carbon source. Supplying carbon
entirely as bicarbonate would require approximately 79 kg/day of sodium
bicarbonate at peak, with a corresponding sodium load that would drive salinity
past the limits of Section 5.2 within weeks.

### 7.3 Injection Design

| Parameter | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Injection method | Carbonation sump or equivalent contacting device |
| Design utilisation | ≥ 65 % |
| Simple surface sparging | Not acceptable as the primary method; utilisation typically 30 – 40 % |
| Control | pH-controlled, on the pond pH signal |
| Injection timing | Daylight hours, matched to photosynthetic demand |
| Night-time injection | Not required; carbon is not fixed in darkness |

Depth of the contacting device is the principal determinant of utilisation, and
the civil design of the sump shall be established during detailed design rather
than left to the CO₂ equipment vendor.

### 7.4 CO₂ Safety

CO₂ is heavier than air and accumulates in low-lying and confined spaces. The
requirements of Volume 1 § 12 apply. Additionally:

- CO₂ storage shall be located outdoors, in a ventilated area, away from
  excavations, sumps and building entries;
- fixed CO₂ detection shall be provided in any enclosed space where CO₂ piping
  or storage is present;
- carbonation sumps shall be treated as confined spaces for entry purposes.

## 8. Inoculum and Culture Scale-Up

### 8.1 Scale-Up Train

| Step | Nominal volume | Environment | Nominal residence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Master culture | 250 mL – 1 L | Laboratory, controlled | Maintained continuously |
| Working culture | 5 – 20 L | Laboratory, controlled | 7 – 14 days |
| Starter tank 1 | 200 L | Closed tank, controlled | 7 – 14 days |
| Starter tank 2 | 2 m³ | Closed tank | 7 – 14 days |
| Nursery pond | 50 m² / 10 m³ | Covered pond | 10 – 20 days |
| Production pond | 500 m² / 100 m³ | Open raceway | Semi-continuous |

Each step shall be approximately a tenfold volume increase. A production pond
shall not be started from a culture more than one step removed from it.

### 8.2 Inoculum Release Criteria

A culture shall not be transferred to the next stage unless it meets all of:

| Criterion | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Microscopic examination | Characteristic *A. platensis* filament morphology dominant |
| Contaminating organisms | Within the limits of Section 16 |
| Grazers | None detected |
| Biomass density | At or above the stated transfer density for the step |
| Filament condition | Intact, motile, no widespread fragmentation or lysis |
| Records | Transfer authorised and recorded against a batch number |

Per Volume 1 § 5.3, culture loss is a critical production risk. The master and
working cultures shall be maintained in duplicate, in physically separate
locations, so that a single incident cannot destroy the facility's entire
genetic starting material.

## 9. Water Balance

### 9.1 Water Quality Requirements

Volume 1 § 10 requires that water be qualified by analysis. For process use the
following are of particular significance to spirulina culture.

| Parameter | Significance | Preliminary limit for make-up water |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total hardness | Scaling; precipitation of carbonates at culture pH | < 300 mg/L as CaCO₃ |
| Calcium | Precipitates as carbonate, consumes alkalinity | < 100 mg/L |
| Magnesium | Nutrient at low level, precipitation risk at high level | < 50 mg/L |
| Chloride | Contributes to salinity accumulation | < 500 mg/L |
| Sulphate | Contributes to salinity accumulation | < 400 mg/L |
| Iron | Nutrient; causes discolouration at excess | < 1 mg/L |
| Heavy metals | Concentrate in biomass; subject to product limits | Confirmed against Section 15 |
| Nitrate | Contributes to nutrient balance; must be accounted, not ignored | Measured and credited |
| Fluoride | Regional groundwater concern in Telangana | Per drinking-water standard |
| Microbiological | Product safety | Per drinking-water standard |
| Pesticide residues | Product safety; agricultural catchment | Screened where catchment warrants |

Groundwater in parts of the Hyderabad region is hard, may be saline and may
carry elevated fluoride. Volume 1 § 8.2 anticipates this. Water treatment
selection shall follow the actual analysis (A-06) and shall not be assumed.

Where the make-up water carries significant hardness, the accumulation of
calcium and magnesium over successive evaporation cycles — not their
concentration in a single batch — is the governing design case.

### 9.2 Water Demand — Stage 1

| Stream | Peak day | Mean day | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pond evaporation make-up | 20.0 m³ | 12.0 m³ | 2,000 m² at 10 / 6 mm/day |
| Water leaving with wet cake | 0.10 m³ | 0.06 m³ | 95 / 55 kg/day |
| Biomass washing | 0.24 m³ | 0.14 m³ | 2 L per kg wet cake |
| Blowdown for salinity control | 2.0 m³ | 2.0 m³ | Section 9.5 |
| Equipment and floor cleaning | 1.5 m³ | 1.0 m³ | Assumed (A-07) |
| Laboratory | 0.2 m³ | 0.2 m³ | Assumed |
| **Process sub-total** | **24.0 m³** | **15.4 m³** | |
| Domestic and potable | 0.9 m³ | 0.9 m³ | 20 persons at 45 L |
| **Total** | **24.9 m³** | **16.3 m³** | |
| **Design raw-water supply** | **30 m³/day** | | Includes margin per Vol 1 § 15 |

Annual process water demand is approximately 5,200 m³.

### 9.3 Evaporation Basis

| Parameter | Preliminary value |
| --- | --- |
| Annual mean pan evaporation, Hyderabad region | ≈ 7 mm/day |
| Pan-to-pond coefficient | 0.75 – 0.85 |
| Design mean pond evaporation | 6 mm/day |
| Design peak pond evaporation | 10 mm/day |

Evaporation is the dominant water demand — approximately 80% of peak process
demand — and the pond-evaporation figure therefore governs the sizing of the
raw-water system, the storage and the abstraction. It shall be confirmed against
site meteorological records (A-09) before the water supply is committed.

### 9.4 Water Storage

Per Volume 1 § 15, water storage shall be a minimum of one day of critical
demand.

| Storage | Minimum | Recommended |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raw water | 30 m³ | 60 m³ (two days) |
| Treated process water | One day of treated demand | Sized on treatment plant turndown |
| Firefighting reserve | Per local fire authority | Not to be drawn for process use |

Two days of raw-water storage is recommended rather than the one-day minimum
because a borewell or supply failure during peak evaporation gives less than 24
hours before pond level, and therefore culture concentration and temperature,
begin to move outside the ranges of Section 5.

### 9.5 Medium Recycling and Blowdown

Filtrate from harvesting is spent culture medium and shall be returned to the
ponds. It carries residual nutrient, alkalinity and dissolved salts.

Recycling conserves water, nutrient and bicarbonate, but concentrates dissolved
salts with every evaporation cycle. Salinity therefore rises continuously unless
a blowdown is taken.

| Parameter | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| Filtrate return | Standard practice, all harvests |
| Salinity control | Blowdown when TDS approaches 30 g/L |
| Preliminary blowdown allowance | 2 m³/day averaged |
| Blowdown disposal | Per Section 20; not to be discharged untreated |

The blowdown rate is a preliminary allowance only. The actual rate is determined
by the dissolved-solids content of the make-up water, which is not yet known,
and shall be recalculated once the water analysis (A-06) is available. Saline
make-up water produces a substantially higher blowdown rate, higher water
consumption and a larger effluent disposal problem — this is the mechanism by
which poor source water raises the cost of the whole facility, and it shall be
evaluated before the water source is committed.

### 9.6 Rainfall and Dilution

Direct rainfall onto an open pond dilutes the culture and the medium.

A 100 mm rainfall event onto a pond operating at 200 mm depth represents a 50%
dilution of alkalinity, salinity and nutrient concentration in a single day. At
the resulting pH and alkalinity the culture's principal contamination barrier
(Section 5.2) is substantially weakened at exactly the moment the culture is
under stress.

The following shall be provided:

| Requirement | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| Freeboard | 150 mm minimum above operating depth |
| Overflow | Controlled overflow weir to the stormwater system, not uncontrolled spill |
| Pre-emptive drawdown | Ability to reduce pond level ahead of a forecast event |
| Post-event correction | Ability to restore alkalinity and salinity by reagent addition |
| Perimeter protection | Site grading per Volume 1 § 8.3 to keep external stormwater out of ponds |

Pond covers shall be evaluated during detailed design against capital cost, wind
loading, light transmission and access for harvesting.

## 10. Harvesting Design Criteria

### 10.1 Harvest Philosophy

Harvesting is semi-continuous. A fraction of the culture volume is withdrawn,
the biomass is separated, and the clarified medium is returned to the same pond.

| Parameter | Peak design value |
| --- | --- |
| Culture harvested | 40.1 m³/day |
| Harvest window | 5 hours |
| Harvest flow rate | 8.0 m³/h |
| Design pump capacity | 10 m³/h (per Vol 1 § 15, ≥ 10% above duty) |
| Harvest concentration | 0.65 g/L |
| Capture efficiency | 92 % |
| Fraction of pond volume per day | 10 % |

Harvesting shall normally be carried out in the late morning, when biomass
density is at its daily maximum and before peak afternoon temperature.

### 10.2 Primary Screening

Spirulina's filamentous morphology — filaments of approximately 100–500 µm —
permits separation by screen filtration. This is the single most important
process advantage of spirulina over single-celled microalgae, which require
centrifugation, and the design shall not discard it by selecting equipment
suited to unicellular algae.

| Parameter | Design basis |
| --- | --- |
| Separation method | Inclined or vibrating screen filtration |
| Screen aperture | 30 – 50 µm |
| Screen material | Food-grade, corrosion resistant in alkaline service |
| Hydraulic loading | 5 – 15 m³/h per m² of screen |
| Minimum screen area at design flow | 1.5 m² |
| Slurry concentration after screening | ≈ 30 g/L (3% solids) |
| Slurry volume, peak | ≈ 800 L/day |
| Centrifugation | Not required, and not to be specified without justification |

Screens shall be arranged for cleaning without tools and shall be inspected for
blinding and for damage at every harvest. A torn screen passes biomass to the
filtrate and the loss is not immediately visible.

### 10.3 Dewatering

| Stage | Inlet solids | Outlet solids | Peak throughput |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary screening | 0.065 % | ≈ 3 % | 40.1 m³/day |
| Dewatering | ≈ 3 % | 10 – 15 % | 800 L/day |
| Pressing | 10 – 15 % | 18 – 22 % | ≈ 200 kg/day |
| **Wet cake to drying** | | **20 % (design)** | **120 kg/day** |

Pressing pressure and duration shall be limited to that required to reach the
target solids. Over-pressing ruptures filaments, releases intracellular material
including phycocyanin into the press liquor, and is visible as a blue-green
tinge in the liquor. Press liquor colour shall be a routine operator check.

### 10.4 Washing

Harvested biomass carries culture medium — principally sodium bicarbonate,
sodium nitrate and sodium chloride. Unwashed biomass produces a product with
elevated ash and sodium and a perceptibly saline taste.

| Parameter | Design basis |
| --- | --- |
| Wash water quality | Treated process water, potable standard |
| Wash ratio | 2 L per kg wet cake (preliminary) |
| Wash stages | To be confirmed by trial against the ash specification |
| Verification | Product ash and sodium per Section 15 |
| Wash water disposal | Saline; per Section 20 |

The wash ratio shall be optimised against the product ash specification. Under-
washing fails the specification; over-washing wastes treated water, increases
saline effluent and begins to leach soluble intracellular components.

### 10.5 Wet Biomass Holding

Wet biomass at 20% solids, at ambient temperature, in an alkaline matrix, is a
highly perishable intermediate.

| Parameter | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Maximum hold time, ambient | 2 hours |
| Maximum hold time, chilled at ≤ 5 °C | 8 hours |
| Preferred | Direct transfer to drying, no intermediate storage |
| Vessel design | Hygienic, fully drainable, cleanable per Volume 1 § 5.4 |

The interval between harvest and the dryer is the point of greatest
microbiological risk in the entire process. Drying capacity shall be sized so
that a peak-day harvest can be dried without accumulating a queue of wet cake,
and the design shall not rely on chilled holding as a routine measure.

## 11. Drying Design Criteria

### 11.1 Basis

| Parameter | Peak design value |
| --- | --- |
| Wet cake feed | 120 kg/day at 20% solids |
| Dry solids | 24 kg/day |
| Product at 5% moisture | 25.3 kg/day |
| Water evaporated | 95 kg/day |
| Drying window | 8 hours |
| Evaporation rate | 11.9 kg water/h |
| Specific energy | 4,500 – 6,000 kJ/kg water evaporated |
| Thermal duty at 5,000 kJ/kg | 16.5 kW |
| Design thermal duty | 20 kW |

### 11.2 Product Temperature

Phycocyanin, the pigment that gives spirulina its colour and a significant part
of its commercial value, is heat labile. Drying is the process step at which
product quality is most readily destroyed.

| Parameter | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Maximum product temperature | 60 °C |
| Maximum product temperature, brief exposure | 65 °C |
| Target final moisture | 5 % |
| Maximum final moisture | 7 % |
| Verification | Product temperature measured, not inferred from air temperature |

Air inlet temperature may exceed the product temperature limit provided that
evaporative cooling maintains the product below it. This holds only while free
water is present. The end of the drying cycle, when evaporative cooling falls
away, is where product temperature runs up, and the control system shall be
designed for that condition rather than for the steady-state.

### 11.3 Dryer Selection

| Option | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Spray drying | Standard at large commercial scale; brief thermal exposure; high capital cost; requires feed at lower solids than the press produces |
| Low-temperature hot-air drying, extruded biomass | Suited to Stage 1 scale; biomass extruded to increase surface area; well-established for spirulina |
| Solar-assisted drying with controlled auxiliary heat | Lowest energy cost; requires enclosure for hygiene and pest control; monsoon performance must be assessed |
| Open sun drying | **Not acceptable.** Uncontrolled contamination, pest access, no temperature control, no traceability |

Volume 1 § 4.3 requires controlled drying. Drying shall be a controlled process
with recorded parameters, whatever the energy source. The selection between the
acceptable options shall be made in detailed design against energy cost, capital
cost, monsoon performance and product quality trial results.

### 11.4 Drying Hygiene

| Requirement | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| Enclosure | Drying shall take place within an enclosed, pest-proofed space |
| Air filtration | Drying air shall be filtered |
| Product contact surfaces | Food-grade, cleanable, per Volume 1 § 5.4 |
| Records | Time, temperature and batch recorded for every drying cycle |

## 12. Milling, Sieving and Blending

| Parameter | Design basis |
| --- | --- |
| Feed | 25.3 kg/day dried biomass, peak |
| Mill type | Impact or pin mill, food-grade |
| Nominal mill capacity | 50 kg/h (well above peak; sized for expansion and turndown) |
| Target particle size | 95% passing 180 µm (80 mesh) |
| Sieving | Integral, with oversize return |
| Temperature rise in milling | Limited; product shall not exceed 60 °C |
| Metal detection | Provided downstream of milling |
| Blending | For homogenisation of a batch prior to sampling and release |

Spirulina powder is hygroscopic. Milling and sieving shall take place in a
humidity-controlled space, and dried product shall not be held in open
containers between drying and packing. Product that reabsorbs moisture above the
Section 15 specification has to be re-dried, with a second thermal exposure and
further pigment loss.

Dust generated in milling is both a hygiene matter and a Volume 1 § 12 safety
matter. Dust extraction shall be provided.

## 16. Contamination Control and Culture Hygiene

### 16.1 Contaminants of Concern

| Type | Examples | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Competing phototrophs | *Chlorella*, *Oscillatoria*, other cyanobacteria | Product purity, possible microcystin |
| Grazers | Rotifers, amoebae, ciliates | Rapid culture collapse |
| Insect larvae | Chironomids, mosquito larvae | Product contamination, grazing |
| Bacteria and fungi | Various | Product microbiology |
| Foreign matter | Dust, wind-blown debris, birds | Product contamination |

### 16.2 Control Measures

The primary barriers are chemical and operational, in this order:

1. **Medium chemistry.** High pH and high alkalinity per Section 5.2. This is
   the principal barrier and it is free — it is the reason the culture chemistry
   limits are treated as contamination controls.
2. **Culture density.** A dense, actively growing culture resists invasion.
3. **Verified inoculum.** Release criteria per Section 8.2.
4. **Physical exclusion.** Pond siting, perimeter management, control of
   wind-blown debris and bird access, per Volume 1 § 5.4.
5. **Pond cleaning and disinfection.** Between campaigns and after any culture
   loss.
6. **Personnel and equipment discipline.** Dedicated equipment per pond where
   practical; no transfer of sampling or cleaning equipment between ponds
   without cleaning.

### 16.3 Monitoring and Action Limits

| Check | Frequency | Action limit | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Culture microscopy | Daily, every production pond | Any grazer detected | Immediate assessment; harvest or dump decision |
| Competing phototrophs | Daily, every production pond | Above 5% of field by area | Increase pH and alkalinity; increase harvest rate |
| Filament morphology | Daily | Widespread fragmentation or lysis | Investigate; check temperature, pH, nutrient |
| Pond surface | Every shift | Foam, scum, colour change, odour | Investigate |
| Insect larvae | Daily | Any detection | Identify source; treat |

Daily microscopy is the single most valuable process control in spirulina
cultivation. A rotifer population can collapse a pond within 48 to 72 hours; it
is detectable by microscope days before it is visible to the eye. The
microbiology laboratory required by Volume 1 § 4.5 exists principally for this
purpose and shall be staffed and equipped accordingly.

### 16.4 Culture Loss Response

A documented procedure shall exist covering:

- criteria for declaring a culture lost;
- isolation of the affected pond;
- safe disposal of the rejected culture, per Section 20;
- cleaning and disinfection before restart;
- restart from the inoculum train per Section 8;
- investigation and recording of cause.

Rejected culture shall not be transferred to another pond in an attempt to save
it.

## 18. Process Control Philosophy

| Function | Control mode |
| --- | --- |
| Pond circulation | Continuous, manual start, alarmed on failure |
| pH / CO₂ injection | Automatic, closed loop on pond pH |
| Nutrient dosing | Manual or semi-automatic, verified by laboratory analysis |
| Make-up water | Automatic on pond level, with high-level cut-off |
| Harvesting | Manual initiation, automatic sequence |
| Dewatering | Semi-automatic |
| Drying | Automatic control on product temperature, recorded |
| Milling and packing | Manual initiation |

Automation is applied where a measurement is reliable, the response is fast and
the consequence of delay is significant — pH and CO₂, dryer temperature, pond
level. It is not applied to nutrient dosing, where the governing measurement is
a laboratory analysis rather than a live signal, and where automatic dosing on
an unverified assumption compounds an error over days before anyone sees it.

Per Volume 1 § 15, the control panel shall carry 20% spare I/O.

### 19.2 Specific Energy

At peak output, approximately 420 kWh/day against 24 kg/day of dry biomass gives
a specific energy of approximately **17.5 kWh per kg of dry biomass**.

This is high. It is characteristic of a small facility with electrical drying,
and it is dominated by two loads:

| Load | Share of daily energy | Reduction route |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drying | 38 % | Solar-assisted or biomass-fired heat in place of electrical |
| Pond circulation | 34 % | Paddle-wheel and channel hydraulic design; bend and baffle geometry |

Volume 1 § 3.5 requires that energy consumption be minimised. Those two items
are where that requirement is met or missed, and both are decided in detailed
design rather than in operation. Circulation energy in particular is set
permanently by the civil geometry of the raceway — channel bends, baffle
placement and paddle-wheel immersion — and cannot be recovered afterwards by
operational effort.

Solar-power integration per Volume 1 § 4.4 shall be assessed against this load
profile. The load is strongly daytime-weighted, which suits solar generation,
with the exception of continuous night-time circulation.

## 20. Effluent and Waste Streams

| Stream | Peak quantity | Character | Disposal route |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Harvest filtrate | 40 m³/day | Alkaline, saline, nutrient-bearing | Returned to ponds |
| Blowdown | 2 m³/day | High pH, high TDS, nutrient-bearing | Requires treatment or consented disposal |
| Wash water | 0.24 m³/day | Saline, low solids | Combined with blowdown |
| Press liquor | ≈ 0.7 m³/day | Nutrient-bearing, may carry pigment | Assess for return to ponds |
| Equipment cleaning water | 1.5 m³/day | Detergent-bearing | Segregated, treated |
| Rejected culture | Up to 100 m³ per event | Alkaline, high biomass | Controlled disposal, Volume 1 § 13 |
| Screen and filter rejects | Minor | Biomass, debris | Solid waste |
| Stormwater | Per drainage design | — | Segregated from process effluent |

Per Volume 1 § 13, no wastewater shall be discharged without determining its pH,
nutrient content, biological load and suitability for the proposed route.

The governing effluent issue is **salinity, not organic load**. Blowdown and
wash water are alkaline and saline. They cannot be discharged to ground or to a
watercourse without consent, and their salinity limits their use for irrigation.
Disposal route shall be established with the Telangana State Pollution Control
Board (A-10) before the water balance is finalised, because a constrained
disposal route forces a lower blowdown rate, which in turn constrains the
acceptable salinity of the make-up water and may change the water treatment
selection entirely.

Rejected culture from a culture-loss event is a large, infrequent, high-pH
discharge. Capacity to hold and neutralise a full pond volume shall be provided
rather than assuming it can be released.
