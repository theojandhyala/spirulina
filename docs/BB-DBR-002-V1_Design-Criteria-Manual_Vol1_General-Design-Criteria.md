## 1. Purpose of the Manual

This Design Criteria Manual establishes the minimum technical, safety, quality, operational and performance requirements for the design and development of the BlueBloom Spirulina Production Facility.

The manual shall be used as the principal engineering reference for:

- feasibility development;
- detailed project reporting;
- front-end engineering design;
- detailed engineering;
- procurement;
- vendor evaluation;
- construction;
- inspection;
- commissioning;
- performance testing;
- operations;
- maintenance;
- future expansion.

Where project drawings, specifications, equipment data sheets or vendor documents conflict with this manual, the more stringent technical requirement shall normally apply unless a formally approved engineering deviation has been issued.

---

## 2. Project Overview

BlueBloom Spirulina Private Limited intends to develop a commercial spirulina cultivation, harvesting, drying, processing and packaging facility in the Hyderabad region of Telangana.

The first development stage shall occupy approximately one acre and shall be configured to allow orderly expansion to two, three and ultimately five acres.

The facility is intended to produce spirulina suitable for:

- bulk food-grade supply;
- branded spirulina powder;
- spirulina tablets;
- nutraceutical formulations;
- premium health and wellness products;
- domestic distribution;
- future export markets;
- potential pharmaceutical-grade development.

The project shall adopt a modular design philosophy so that ponds, utilities, harvesting systems, drying systems, laboratories, packaging equipment and electrical infrastructure can be expanded without extensive reconstruction of the initial facility.

---

## 3. Project Objectives

The principal project objectives are:

1. To establish a technically reliable commercial spirulina facility suited to the Hyderabad climate.
2. To maintain consistent biomass quality and minimise contamination risks.
3. To provide controlled cultivation conditions for pH, temperature, nutrient concentration, circulation, alkalinity and biomass density.
4. To design the initial facility for efficient future expansion.
5. To minimise energy, water and nutrient consumption.
6. To provide safe and hygienic working conditions.
7. To support compliance with applicable Indian food, environmental, labour and industrial requirements.
8. To develop an auditable production and quality-control system.
9. To enable batch traceability from pond inoculation through final packaging.
10. To provide the engineering foundation for future export certification and higher-grade production.

## 5. Design Philosophy

### 5.1 General Philosophy

The facility shall be designed to achieve safe, hygienic, maintainable and commercially practical operation.

The design shall avoid unnecessary complexity while retaining sufficient automation and monitoring to maintain consistent cultivation and product quality.

Equipment and materials shall be selected based on:

- suitability for spirulina production;
- food-contact compatibility;
- local availability;
- ease of maintenance;
- energy efficiency;
- corrosion resistance;
- expected service life;
- availability of spare parts;
- vendor support in India;
- whole-life cost.

### 5.2 Modular Expansion

The one-acre facility shall represent the first module of a larger development.

Where economically justified, the initial project shall provide common infrastructure sized for future expansion, including:

- site roads;
- main drainage;
- incoming electrical supply;
- transformer capacity;
- water storage;
- laboratory facilities;
- control-system architecture;
- central utility corridors;
- administrative facilities;
- finished-product warehousing;
- future process-equipment space.

Underground utilities and main pipe corridors shall be positioned to minimise disturbance during future construction.

### 5.3 Reliability

Critical cultivation systems shall be designed to minimise the risk of culture loss.

Particular attention shall be given to:

- paddle-wheel reliability;
- power interruption;
- water availability;
- nutrient dosing;
- pH control;
- temperature excursions;
- contamination;
- harvesting interruptions;
- drying-equipment failure.

Critical equipment shall have duty and standby capacity where justified by production risk.

### 5.4 Hygiene

All product-contact surfaces shall be hygienically designed.

The design shall minimise:

- stagnant liquid pockets;
- inaccessible cleaning areas;
- cross-contamination;
- pest access;
- dust accumulation;
- standing wastewater;
- uncontrolled personnel movement;
- mixing of raw and finished products.

### 5.5 Maintainability

Equipment shall be arranged to allow safe access for:

- inspection;
- lubrication;
- cleaning;
- calibration;
- component replacement;
- motor removal;
- gearbox servicing;
- valve operation;
- instrument maintenance.

Maintenance access shall not require routine entry into hazardous or contaminated areas unless no practical alternative exists.

## 8. Site Conditions

### 8.1 General

Final design conditions shall be confirmed using:

- registered topographical survey;
- geotechnical investigation;
- water-quality analysis;
- flood-risk assessment;
- electrical-supply assessment;
- local meteorological data;
- drainage study;
- environmental screening.

### 8.2 Preliminary Site Basis

Until site-specific studies are completed, the design shall assume:

- a warm semi-arid to tropical regional climate;
- high solar exposure;
- seasonal monsoon rainfall;
- elevated summer temperatures;
- moderate dust exposure;
- potential short-duration intense rainfall;
- variable groundwater quality;
- potential hardness or salinity in borewell water.

### 8.3 Site Elevation and Grading

The production area shall be graded to prevent stormwater from entering cultivation ponds, process buildings or chemical-storage areas.

Finished floor and pond-edge levels shall be established above the selected design flood level.

Where practical, gravity drainage shall be used for non-product wastewater and stormwater.

## 10. Water Design Criteria

Water is a critical process input and shall be evaluated for:

- pH;
- total dissolved solids;
- alkalinity;
- hardness;
- chlorides;
- sulphates;
- iron;
- manganese;
- fluoride;
- nitrate;
- ammonia;
- heavy metals;
- microbiological contamination;
- pesticide residues where relevant.

Water treatment shall be selected based on actual analysis.

Potential treatment stages may include:

- raw-water screening;
- sediment filtration;
- activated-carbon filtration;
- softening;
- reverse osmosis;
- ultraviolet treatment;
- chlorination followed by dechlorination;
- micron filtration.

No water source shall be approved for production use solely on the basis of visual appearance.

## 11. Electrical Design Basis

The facility shall normally be designed for the local Telangana electrical distribution system.

The electrical design shall provide for:

- incoming utility supply;
- transformer where required;
- main low-voltage switchboard;
- motor control centres;
- variable-frequency drives;
- distribution boards;
- emergency circuits;
- earthing;
- lightning protection;
- solar-power integration;
- backup generation;
- uninterruptible power supply for control and laboratory systems.

Critical pond-circulation equipment shall be connected to backup power where a power interruption could result in significant biological or commercial loss.

## 12. Safety Philosophy

The facility shall be designed to protect:

- employees;
- contractors;
- visitors;
- consumers;
- equipment;
- product;
- the surrounding environment.

The design shall address:

- rotating machinery;
- electrical hazards;
- wet working areas;
- chemical handling;
- compressed gas;
- carbon dioxide exposure;
- confined spaces;
- manual handling;
- work at height;
- hot surfaces;
- dust;
- fire;
- biological contamination;
- vehicle movement.

Machinery guards shall be provided for paddle wheels, couplings, shafts, belts, pulleys and other moving components.

Emergency stops shall be accessible from normal operating positions.

---

## 13. Environmental Design Philosophy

The project shall minimise environmental impact through:

- efficient water use;
- nutrient recovery where practical;
- controlled disposal of rejected culture;
- safe management of cleaning chemicals;
- separation of stormwater and process wastewater;
- prevention of contaminated discharge;
- energy-efficient equipment;
- solar-power integration;
- waste segregation;
- recycling of suitable packaging and process materials.

Wastewater shall not be discharged without determining its pH, nutrient content, biological load and suitability for the proposed disposal route.

## 15. Engineering Safety Factors

Unless otherwise justified by calculation, conservative design allowances shall be included for:

- future production expansion;
- pond evaporation;
- process losses;
- pump head;
- pipe friction;
- motor power;
- electrical cable capacity;
- structural loading;
- water storage;
- nutrient storage;
- equipment throughput;
- warehouse capacity.

Typical preliminary allowances are:

| Design item | Minimum preliminary allowance |
| --- | --- |
| Pump flow capacity | 10% above calculated duty |
| Pump differential head | 10–15% above calculated duty |
| Motor power | Next standard size above maximum duty |
| Process piping flow | 15% future allowance |
| Electrical feeder capacity | 20% spare capacity |
| Control-panel I/O | 20% spare channels |
| Warehouse space | 15% operational allowance |
| Water storage | Minimum one day of critical demand |
| Critical chemical storage | Minimum seven days, subject to safety limits |

These allowances shall not replace detailed engineering calculations.

## 16. Design Deviations

Any proposed departure from this manual shall be documented through a formal Design Deviation Request.

Each request shall include:

- reference to the affected criterion;
- reason for deviation;
- technical justification;
- safety impact;
- quality impact;
- cost impact;
- schedule impact;
- operational impact;
- proposed mitigation;
- approval signatures.

No verbal agreement shall constitute an approved design deviation.
