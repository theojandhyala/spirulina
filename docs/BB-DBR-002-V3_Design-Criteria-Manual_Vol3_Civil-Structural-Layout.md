## 3. Site Investigation Requirements

Volume 1 § 8.1 requires that final design conditions be confirmed by survey and
investigation. This section defines the minimum scope.

### 3.1 Topographical Survey

| Item | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Type | Registered topographical survey |
| Grid | 10 m maximum, closer where ground is irregular |
| Vertical datum | Established permanent benchmark on site |
| Extent | Full 5-acre master plan area, not only the Stage 1 acre |
| Features | Boundaries, existing structures, trees, services, access, drainage paths, adjacent levels |
| Accuracy | ±25 mm vertical on hard surfaces |

The survey shall cover the whole 5-acre holding. Surveying only the Stage 1 acre
prevents the master plan required by Volume 1 § 20 from being set out correctly
and is a false economy.

### 3.2 Geotechnical Investigation

| Item | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Boreholes, building areas | Minimum 3, or 1 per 1,000 m² of built area, whichever is greater |
| Borehole depth | 1.5 × foundation width below founding level, or 3 m into sound rock |
| Trial pits, pond areas | 30 m grid, minimum 2.5 m depth or refusal |
| In-situ testing | Standard Penetration Test per IS 2131; plate load test per IS 1888 where warranted |
| Classification | Per IS 1498 |
| Laboratory testing | Per IS 2720 series |
| Groundwater | Standing level recorded, with seasonal variation assessed |

Laboratory testing shall include, as a minimum: grading and Atterberg limits;
free swell index; shear strength; consolidation; compaction (Proctor);
permeability of pond subgrade; and the chemical tests of Section 3.4.

### 3.3 Matters Specific to This Site

The Hyderabad region lies on the Deccan plateau, in predominantly granitic
terrain. Three conditions shall be specifically investigated and reported:

| Condition | Why it matters here |
| --- | --- |
| **Depth to rock** | Shallow weathered or sound granite is common. Rock excavation is slow and expensive, and it governs whether ponds are formed by cutting into grade or by building embankments above it — a decision that changes the earthworks cost of the whole project |
| **Expansive soil** | Where clays of high plasticity are present, seasonal shrink-swell will distort a shallow pond floor and crack a rigid liner support. Free swell index shall be determined at every trial pit |
| **Groundwater level** | Governs uplift on an empty lined pond, per Section 8.5, and buoyancy on below-grade structures such as the carbonation sump and harvest sump |

Where rock is shallow, the design shall favour raising ponds on engineered
embankments over excavating into rock. Where the two are comparable in cost,
the embankment option is preferred: it keeps the pond floor above groundwater
and removes the uplift case entirely.

### 3.4 Chemical Testing of Soil and Groundwater

| Test | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| pH of soil and groundwater | Concrete and buried-metal durability |
| Sulphate content, soil and groundwater | Concrete exposure classification per IS 456 Table 4 |
| Chloride content | Reinforcement corrosion risk |
| Organic content | Foundation suitability |
| Resistivity | Buried-services corrosion protection |

Sulphate results shall be reported before concrete mix design is fixed, since
they may require sulphate-resisting or pozzolanic cement.

### 3.5 Hydrological and Drainage Study

| Item | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Catchment | External catchment draining towards the site, delineated |
| Design flood level | Established and recorded |
| Existing drainage paths | Mapped, with the discharge point identified |
| Rainfall data | Site-relevant IMD records, including short-duration intensities |
| Design storm | Per Section 5.2 |

## 4. Site Development and Grading

### 4.1 Grading Philosophy

Per Volume 1 § 8.3, the production area shall be graded so that stormwater
cannot enter cultivation ponds, process buildings or chemical storage areas.

| Requirement | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| Surface water | Directed away from ponds, buildings and stores by grade, not by pumping |
| Gravity drainage | Used for non-product wastewater and stormwater wherever practical |
| Cut and fill | Balanced across the site where ground conditions permit |
| Topsoil | Stripped, stockpiled and re-used in landscaping and embankment faces |
| Pond block | Graded level within the tolerances of Section 7.3 |

### 4.2 Levels and Flood Protection

| Level | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Finished floor level, all buildings | Minimum 300 mm above adjacent finished ground level, and above design flood level |
| Pond crest level | Above design flood level, per Volume 1 § 8.3 |
| Chemical and nutrient store floor | As buildings, plus bund per Section 10.6 |
| Electrical rooms and substation | Above design flood level with additional freeboard |

### 4.3 Earthworks and Compaction

| Element | Compaction requirement |
| --- | --- |
| General site fill | 95% of IS 2720 Part 7 maximum dry density |
| Fill beneath buildings and hardstanding | 98% of maximum dry density |
| Pond embankment fill | 95% minimum, in layers not exceeding 200 mm compacted |
| Pond floor subgrade | 95% minimum, proof-rolled |
| Backfill to services | 95%, in layers not exceeding 200 mm |

Fill shall be free of organic matter, deleterious material and stones exceeding
75 mm, and shall not exceed 10 mm in the top 150 mm of pond subgrade per
Section 8.2.

Expansive soil shall not be used as fill beneath ponds, buildings or pavements.

## 5. Site Drainage

### 5.1 Philosophy

Three drainage systems shall be provided and shall remain **physically separate
throughout**, per Volume 1 § 13:

| System | Content | Destination |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stormwater | Clean rainfall runoff from roofs, roads and open areas | Site discharge point, subject to consent |
| Process effluent | Blowdown, wash water, cleaning water, rejected culture | Effluent management per Volume 2 § 20 |
| Foul drainage | Personnel facilities | Septic system or municipal connection |

Cross-connection between these systems shall not be provided under any
circumstance, including for emergency relief. A single cross-connection converts
a contained process discharge into an uncontrolled environmental release and
defeats the segregation requirement at its only point of failure.

### 5.2 Design Storm

| Element | Design return period |
| --- | --- |
| Roof drainage | 1 in 25 years |
| Internal site drainage, roads and paved areas | 1 in 25 years |
| Main site outfall and culverts | 1 in 50 years |
| Pond overflow and freeboard check | 1 in 100 years, 24-hour event |
| Flood protection levels | Per design flood level, Section 3.5 |

Rainfall intensity shall be taken from IMD records for the Hyderabad region.
Preliminary design may adopt 75 mm/h for the 1-in-25-year short-duration
intensity, to be confirmed (A-15).

The Hyderabad region has recorded 24-hour rainfall well in excess of 200 mm in
recent extreme events. The pond freeboard check of Section 7.9 shall be carried
out against site records rather than against a typical monsoon day.

### 5.3 Pond Area Drainage

| Requirement | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| Perimeter interception | Cut-off drain upslope of the pond block, discharging to stormwater |
| Inter-pond areas | Graded and drained so water does not pond against embankment toes |
| Embankment crest | Cross-fall away from the pond, so crest runoff does not wash into the culture |
| Pond overflow | Piped to stormwater, not spilled onto the embankment face |

Crest cross-fall shall be away from the water. Runoff from a pond crest carries
dust, soil and whatever has been walked onto it, directly into the culture at
the moment the culture is already being diluted by rain.

## 6. Site Layout and Zoning

### 6.1 Zoning Principles

The site shall be zoned so that material flows one way, from raw input to
finished product, without back-tracking:

| Zone | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| Cultivation | Production ponds, nursery ponds, inoculum building |
| Primary processing | Harvest, dewatering, washing |
| Secondary processing | Drying, milling, sieving, blending |
| Packing and finished goods | Packing, finished-product warehouse, dispatch |
| Support | Laboratory, workshop, stores, utilities |
| Administration and welfare | Office, personnel facilities, security |

Per Volume 1 § 5.4, the design shall prevent mixing of raw and finished
products and shall control personnel movement. Finished-product areas shall not
be accessible directly from cultivation or harvest areas without passing through
a hygiene transition.

### 6.2 Flow Separation

| Flow | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Product | One-directional, cultivation → dispatch, no back-tracking |
| Personnel | Through changing and hygiene facilities before entering product areas |
| Visitors | Routed without entry to product areas |
| Vehicles, inbound | To chemical, nutrient and packaging stores |
| Vehicles, outbound | To finished-goods dispatch |
| Waste | Segregated route, not crossing finished-product routes |

Inbound and outbound vehicle routes shall not require reversing across a
pedestrian route.

### 6.3 Orientation

| Consideration | Design response |
| --- | --- |
| Wind fetch across ponds | Pond long axis preferably perpendicular to prevailing wind, to limit fetch and wave action to the channel width |
| Dust sources | Roads, unpaved areas and vehicle movement located downwind of ponds where possible |
| Chemical store | Located downwind of personnel and product areas |
| Solar access | Ponds free of shading from buildings, tanks and trees, in all seasons |
| Drying equipment | Solar-assisted drying oriented per equipment requirement |

The prevailing wind direction shall be established from site-relevant records
(A-16). Orientation is fixed permanently at layout stage; wave overtopping and
wind-driven scum accumulation cannot be corrected afterwards.

Shading shall be checked at the winter solstice, when shadows are longest, and
not only at the equinox.

### 6.5 Finding — Net Pond Area Against Volume 2 Assumption A-01

**CLOSED at BB-DBR-002-V2 Rev. 1.** Volume 2 assumed a net pond area of
2,000 m² at Stage 1 (A-01). The layout budget of Section 6.4 supports 1,750 m².
Resolution 1 below was adopted: Volume 2 was revised to Rev. 1 against 1,750 m²
and a 3.9 t/year Stage 1 output, and assumption A-01 is closed.

| Item | Volume 2, A-01 | This volume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Net pond area | 2,000 m² | 1,750 m² |
| Culture volume | 400 m³ | 350 m³ |
| Peak dry biomass | 24.0 kg/day | 21.0 kg/day |
| Annual mean dry biomass | 14.0 kg/day | 12.25 kg/day |
| Annual dry biomass | 4.48 t | 3.92 t |
| Peak harvest volume | 40.1 m³/day | 35.1 m³/day |
| Peak wet cake | 120 kg/day | 105 kg/day |

The shortfall is 12.5% of capacity. Three resolutions are available:

1. **Accept 1,750 m².** Revise Volume 2 to Rev. 1 against a 3.9 t/year Stage 1
   output. Equipment already sized on 2,000 m² carries a 14% margin, which is
   not wasted — it becomes usable headroom against the productivity assumptions
   A-03 and A-04.
2. **Recover area by layout optimisation.** Two-storey construction for the
   office, laboratory and personnel block, reduced perimeter setback where
   planning permits, and narrower inter-pond access can recover approximately
   150–200 m², bringing net pond area to 1,900–1,950 m². This adds cost and
   reduces circulation space.
3. **Reduce non-pond area by deferring buildings to Stage 2.** Not recommended.
   Volume 1 § 5.2 requires common infrastructure to be built at Stage 1
   precisely so that it is not rebuilt later.

**Resolution 1 is recommended.** The capacity difference is smaller than the
uncertainty in the productivity assumptions it would be measured against, and
buying it back with two-storey construction and minimum setbacks spends real
capital and permanent circulation space to chase a number that the cultivation
trial may move further in either direction.

This finding was resolved by revising Volume 2 in full to Rev. 1, not by
adjusting the pond area in one volume alone. Volume 2 § 4.5 records the one
deliberate divergence that remains: process equipment is enquired against
24 kg/day, the superseded basis, as margin against the unconfirmed productivity
assumptions A-03 and A-04.

## 7. Raceway Pond Civil Design

### 7.1 Configuration

| Parameter | Stage 1 basis |
| --- | --- |
| Number of production ponds | 4 |
| Net water surface area, total | 1,750 m² (per Section 6.5) |
| Nominal water surface per pond | 435 m² |
| Indicative overall pond dimensions | ≈ 57 m × 8.0 m |
| Channel arrangement | Two channels per pond, separated by a central baffle wall |
| Indicative channel width | ≈ 3.85 m |
| Operating culture depth | 200 mm |
| Freeboard | 150 mm minimum |
| Total wall height above floor | 350 mm minimum, plus embankment where applicable |
| Nursery ponds | 2 × 50 m² |

Final dimensions shall be set in detailed design. The pond count of four is a
requirement, not a preference: Volume 2 § 4.4 fixes it to cap the loss from a
single culture failure at 25% of standing stock and to permit staggered
harvesting.

### 7.2 Construction Options

| Option | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Compacted earth embankment with geomembrane liner | Lowest capital cost; embankment maintenance; liner fully load-bearing on subgrade; suited to level ground |
| Masonry walls, cement rendered, with liner | Moderate cost; well-established in Indian practice; durable crest for access |
| RCC channel walls with earth or blinding floor, with liner | Higher cost; best dimensional control; longest life |
| Full RCC pond, no liner | Highest cost; requires a food-safe, crack-free finish maintained for 20 years; not recommended |

The full RCC unlined option is not recommended. Volume 1 § 6 gives pond civil
works a 20-year design life, and maintaining a crack-free, food-contact concrete
surface in direct contact with the culture for that period is a more demanding
requirement than replacing a liner twice.

Selection shall be made in detailed design against ground conditions, rock
depth per Section 3.3, and whole-life cost per Volume 1 § 5.1.

### 7.3 Governing Tolerance — Pond Floor Level

**Floor level is the governing civil tolerance of this project.**

The culture operates at 200 mm depth. A floor level error of ±20 mm is a ±10%
variation in local culture depth, and therefore in local light path, thermal
mass and areal productivity. A 50 mm error across a pond produces a 25% depth
variation, shallow zones that overheat, and deep zones that never reach the
design velocity.

| Element | Tolerance |
| --- | --- |
| **Pond floor, level across the whole pond** | **±15 mm** |
| Pond floor, local surface irregularity under a 3 m straightedge | ±10 mm |
| Wall top level | ±10 mm |
| Baffle wall alignment | ±20 mm over pond length |
| Channel width | ±25 mm |
| Overall pond dimensions | ±50 mm |

The pond floor shall be surveyed on a 5 m grid after construction and before
lining, and again after lining. Results shall be recorded and accepted before
the pond is filled. A pond floor outside tolerance shall be corrected before
lining, not accepted and compensated for by running at greater depth.

The floor shall be constructed level, not to a fall. Drainage is by the sump of
Section 7.7, not by a graded floor: a floor laid to a fall gives a culture
depth that varies systematically along the pond.

### 7.4 Channel Geometry, Bends and Flow

Volume 2 § 19.2 identifies pond circulation as approximately one third of
facility energy consumption, and states that circulation energy is set
permanently by civil geometry. This section is where that cost is determined.

| Element | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Bend form | Semicircular or near-semicircular, not square |
| Flow deflectors at bends | Provided, to reduce head loss and eliminate dead zones |
| Baffle wall end form | Rounded, not square-ended |
| Channel cross-section | Uniform; no local constrictions, steps or projections |
| Wall face finish | Smooth, to minimise friction |
| Floor finish | Smooth and free of local depressions where biomass can settle |
| Dead zones | None permitted; verified by dye or float testing at commissioning |

Square-ended baffle walls and un-deflected bends are the two most common causes
of both excess paddle-wheel power and biomass settlement in raceway ponds. A
settled deposit at a bend is anaerobic within days and becomes a persistent
contamination source that no amount of operational effort will clear while the
geometry that created it remains.

Circulation velocity shall be verified by measurement at commissioning against
the 0.20–0.30 m/s range of Volume 2 § 5.5, at not fewer than six points per
pond including both bends.

### 7.5 Paddle Wheel Civil Interface

| Element | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Foundation | Independent of the pond wall where practical, to isolate vibration |
| Foundation design | For dynamic loading, continuous duty, per Section 9 |
| Clearance, blade to floor | 20–30 mm, to limit bypass |
| Clearance, blade to walls | 20–30 mm |
| Floor beneath the wheel | Locally reinforced and finished to close tolerance |
| Access | Safe access for inspection, lubrication and motor and gearbox removal, per Volume 1 § 5.5 |
| Guarding | Per Volume 1 § 12 and Volume 2 § 21 |
| Drainage | Local area drained; no standing water at the drive |

Blade clearance is a civil tolerance as much as a mechanical one. It cannot be
achieved unless the floor beneath the wheel is built to the tolerance of Section
7.3, and it shall be verified before the drive is commissioned.

### 7.6 Carbonation Sump

Volume 2 § 7.3 requires a carbonation sump or equivalent contacting device, and
states that sump depth is the principal determinant of CO₂ utilisation, and that
its civil design shall be established in detailed design rather than left to the
CO₂ equipment vendor.

| Parameter | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Form | Local deepening of the channel floor |
| Indicative depth below floor | 1.0 – 1.5 m, to be confirmed against the required 65% utilisation |
| Position | On a straight, not at a bend |
| Hydraulics | No obstruction to channel flow; no dead zone at the sump mouth |
| Structure | Watertight, designed per IS 3370 |
| Uplift | Checked against groundwater per Section 3.3 |
| Drainage | Fully drainable for cleaning |
| Access | Confined-space entry provisions, per Volume 2 § 7.4 |
| Sediment | Accessible for removal; the sump is a natural settlement point |

The sump is a permanent civil structure supporting a process performance
guarantee. Building it shallower than required cannot be corrected later without
breaking out and reconstructing the pond floor.

### 7.7 Draw-off, Drainage and Sumps

| Element | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Harvest draw-off | Positioned in flowing culture, not in a dead zone; screened against debris |
| Pond drainage | Every pond independently and fully drainable, per Volume 2 § 4.4 |
| Drain sump | Local low point, sized for complete emptying |
| Drain-down time | A full pond emptied within one working shift |
| Drain destination | Process effluent system, per Section 5.1 |
| Filtrate return | Return point remote from the draw-off, to prevent short-circuiting |
| Isolation | Each pond independently isolatable |

Filtrate returning immediately adjacent to the draw-off short-circuits the
harvest, so the harvest runs longer, pumps more volume and captures less
biomass per cubic metre than designed.

### 7.8 Embankments and Access

| Element | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Internal side slope, earth embankment | 1 in 2 or flatter, subject to stability analysis |
| External side slope | 1 in 2 or flatter |
| Crest width, pedestrian access | 750 mm minimum |
| Crest width, vehicle access | 3.5 m minimum where required |
| Crest surface | Firm, non-dusting, cross-falling away from the pond |
| Erosion protection | External faces protected against monsoon rainfall |
| Stability | Analysed for rapid drawdown, the governing case for a pond emptied for cleaning |

Rapid drawdown is the governing embankment stability case and is easily
overlooked: the embankment is at its most vulnerable at the moment a pond is
emptied.

### 7.9 Freeboard and Overflow

| Element | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Freeboard | 150 mm minimum above operating depth, per Volume 2 § 9.6 |
| Freeboard check | Against the 1-in-100-year 24-hour event, per Section 5.2 |
| Overflow | Controlled weir, piped to stormwater, per Volume 2 § 9.6 |
| Overflow capacity | The design storm, without the water level reaching the crest |
| Uncontrolled spill over the crest | Not permitted; erodes the embankment and washes culture to ground |
| Wave allowance | Included in freeboard, based on fetch per Section 6.3 |

## 8. Pond Lining

### 8.1 Liner Selection

| Parameter | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Material | Geomembrane, food-contact grade |
| Preferred materials | HDPE or LLDPE, UV-stabilised with carbon black |
| Minimum thickness | 0.75 mm; 1.0 mm preferred for exposed service |
| Food contact | Certified suitable for food contact; certification held on file |
| UV resistance | Suitable for permanent exposed service in high solar exposure per Volume 1 § 9 |
| Chemical resistance | Suitable for pH 9.5–10.5 and the medium of Volume 2 § 6.1 |
| Design life | 8–15 years per Volume 1 § 6, dependent on material |
| Colour | To be considered against culture thermal behaviour |

Food-contact certification is a firm requirement, not a preference. The liner is
a product-contact surface under Volume 1 § 5.4 for the whole life of the
culture. General-purpose pond or canal liner shall not be substituted on cost
grounds.

### 8.2 Subgrade Preparation

| Requirement | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Compaction | Per Section 4.3 |
| Surface | Smooth, rolled, free of ruts and depressions |
| Maximum particle size, top 150 mm | 10 mm |
| Roots, organic matter, debris | Removed |
| Protrusions | None |
| Geotextile cushion | 200–300 g/m² where subgrade is granular or contains any stone |
| Acceptance | Subgrade inspected and accepted in writing before lining begins |

Subgrade defects are the dominant cause of liner puncture, and every one of them
is buried and invisible the moment the liner is laid.

### 8.3 Anchorage

| Element | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Anchor trench | Continuous at the crest of all embankments and walls |
| Indicative trench size | 500 mm × 500 mm |
| Backfill | Compacted; no stone in contact with the liner |
| Wall-mounted fixing | Mechanical batten fixing where anchoring into masonry or concrete |
| Penetrations | Minimised; all sealed with proprietary boots or flanged details |

Every penetration is a potential leak for the life of the liner. Draw-off, drain
and return connections shall be grouped and detailed on the drawings, not
improvised on site.

### 8.4 Seaming and Testing

| Requirement | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| Seaming method | Thermal wedge welding for primary seams; extrusion welding for detail work |
| Operator qualification | Trial welds tested at the start of each shift and on any change of conditions |
| Non-destructive testing | 100% of seams, by air-pressure test of the dual-track weld or vacuum box |
| Destructive testing | Sampled per the inspection and test plan, peel and shear tested |
| Records | Full weld and test records retained as part of the handover documentation |
| Leak survey | Pond filled with water and monitored before the culture is introduced |

A leak found by falling water level after the pond has been inoculated costs a
culture as well as a repair.

### 8.5 Underdrainage and Venting

Where groundwater may rise above pond floor level, or where gas may be generated
in the subgrade, an empty lined pond is subject to uplift. The liner will lift,
billow and can tear.

| Requirement | Applies when |
| --- | --- |
| Granular drainage layer beneath the liner | Groundwater may approach floor level |
| Vent points through the embankment | All ponds, as good practice |
| Passive relief | Provided so that the liner cannot be lifted by trapped water or gas |
| Uplift check | Carried out for the empty pond condition, against the highest recorded groundwater level |

The empty-pond case governs. A pond in service is held down by 200 mm of
culture, which is a very small pressure. Ponds are emptied for cleaning at the
end of the monsoon, when groundwater is at its highest.

### 8.6 Liner Replacement

Volume 1 § 6 gives pond civil works a 20-year design life and liners 8–15
years. **The liner will therefore be replaced at least once, and probably twice,
within the life of the pond structure.**

The civil design shall make replacement possible without demolition:

| Requirement | Basis |
| --- | --- |
| Anchor trenches | Re-excavatable, not capped with concrete or paving |
| Penetrations | Demountable, not cast in |
| Crest access | Adequate for plant and material handling during replacement |
| Fixed items over the liner | Avoided, or removable |
| Records | As-built liner drawings, material certificates and weld records retained for the life of the pond |

Detailing that makes the first liner slightly cheaper and every subsequent
replacement a demolition exercise is a false economy over a 20-year structure.

| Element | Nominal cover |
| --- | --- |

| Liquid-retaining structures | 45 mm, per IS 3370 |

| Slabs, moderate exposure | 30 mm |

Crack width in liquid-retaining structures shall be limited per IS 3370.

**A note on chemical attack.** The culture medium is alkaline at pH 9.5–10.5.
Alkalinity at this level is not aggressive to concrete, which is itself strongly
alkaline. The aggressive constituents are the **sulphates** in the medium —
magnesium sulphate and potassium sulphate per Volume 2 § 6.1 — and any chloride
present.

Where the liner is intact, the culture does not contact the concrete at all, and
the liner is the primary durability barrier. The concrete specification exists to
cover the case where the liner has failed and has not yet been found. It shall
be specified on that basis, and the sulphate content of the final medium
formulation shall be determined and the exposure classified against IS 456
Table 4 (A-18).
